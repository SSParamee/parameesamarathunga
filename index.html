About Me

A user guide. A walkthrough video. An in-app tooltip. A course module. I build all of these. Not across different careers, in the same one.

The question I keep coming back to is simple. "Why do people finish content and still not know what to do next?" Recognizing something is not the same as understanding it. Understanding it is not the same as applying it. Applying it is not the same as making it your own. The gap between knowing and doing is where I chose to work.

Technical writing gives people an accurate place to start. Content strategy makes sure they find that place, in the right format, at the right moment. Content writing earns their attention long enough to matter. Learning design closes the distance between knowing and doing.

Seven years. Four disciplines. Different companies. I do not measure content by how well it reads. I measure it by what people can do when it is read and closed.

WSO2 · Open Banking Accelerator 3.0
Technical Overviews - Consent Authorization & DCR Tryout
Public docs · ob-docs.wso2.com · 2021
Scope

WSO2 Open Banking Accelerator had two workflows that no single well-written article covered: Consent Authorization (authorization endpoints, PAR, identifier-first auth) and Dynamic Client Registration. I wrote both from scratch. The DCR Tryout guide ran 454 lines and walked developers through full API consumer registration using cURL examples, configuration tables, and environment setup.

Challenges
  • OAuth2 PAR and redirect-based authorization look similar on the surface but fork into completely different configuration sequences. Writing them in one article without making developers second-guess which path they were on required a deliberate information architecture decision.
  • The DCR guide had to hold up across three separate runtimes: Identity Server, API Manager, and Streaming Integrator. Every step had to reproduce correctly, not just read correctly.
  • Configuration blocks in open banking docs are long. Collapsing everything hides what developers actually need during a live deployment. Showing everything at once buries the narrative. I decided, block by block, what belongs inline and what goes behind a "Click to see."
Approach

I organized both articles around what a developer needs to do, not how the system is designed. The Consent Authorization article opens at the flow a developer triggers first, then moves toward PAR and identifier-first auth as complexity builds. The DCR guide applies layered disclosure throughout: each section frames the context before the configuration detail opens up. Collapsible blocks for long config kept the page readable without stripping anything a partner deployment would reference.

Outcome

Both articles are live on ob-docs.wso2.com and picked up in partner deployment references. The DCR Tryout guide documented the full API consumer registration flow in 454 lines. Commit history is public under SSParamee on the WSO2 Open Banking GitHub repository.

10+ Websites · 2023 - Present
Framework

Search now operates across three distinct surfaces (organic rankings, AI-generated answers, and featured snippets), each with different structural requirements. I build for all three from the brief, not as an afterthought.


That means organising content around the entities that matter to both algorithms and decision-makers: service, location, credentials, the people behind the business.
